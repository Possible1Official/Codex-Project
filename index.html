Inside the model · equations, strategy policies, and limits

This is an illustrative quarterly planning model, with 100,000 eligible tasks. Each successful task creates $1 of value; an unresolved failure costs $6; reviewing a task costs $0.35. Review catches 90% of failures in this example.

  • Accelerate: full reach, no reliability uplift, $2,000 investment.
  • Controlled pilot: 25% reach, at least 60% review, $1,000 investment.
  • Strengthen first: 65% quarterly reach, +4 percentage points reliability (capped at 100%), $8,000 investment.

Tasks = 100,000 × adoption × strategy reach
Failures = tasks × (1 − reliability) × (1 − review × 0.90)
Net = (tasks − failures) × $1 − tasks × cost − tasks × review × $0.35 − failures × $6 − investment

Adoption and reliability are sampled independently from triangular distributions around your central inputs (±12 and ±4 percentage points); task cost uses ±25%. Bounds are clipped to the slider limits. Human oversight is held fixed. All three strategies share each draw so comparisons face the same sampled conditions.

Constants, policies, and uncertainty widths are invented examples. This model excludes correlations, catastrophic events, learning effects, repeat attempts, revenue dynamics, review capacity, and later-quarter benefits. It calculates expected task counts within each draw, not individual task events. It is a way to examine assumptions, not a validated forecast or recommendation.
